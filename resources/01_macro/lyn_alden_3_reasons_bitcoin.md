# 3 Reasons I'm Investing in Bitcoin

**Lyn Alden | Originally published: July 16, 2020**

> Source: https://www.lynalden.com/invest-in-bitcoin/

---

Blockchain-based cryptocurrencies have been around for over a decade, since the release of Bitcoin in early 2009. While the asset class has grown considerably, it remains relatively small and highly volatile.

I turned bullish on Bitcoin in April 2020 at ~$6,900. As a generalist investor with a value-slant and global macro emphasis, these are the three main reasons:

---

## Reason 1: Scarcity + Network Effect

Bitcoin is an open-source peer-to-peer software monetary system with a hard cap of **21 million coins**. No central authority can change that limit.

### What makes it valuable?

Satoshi Nakamoto described it as a rare digital commodity:

> "Imagine there was a base metal as scarce as gold but with one special, magical property: can be transported over a communications channel."

Bitcoin has the properties of money: scarce, durable, portable, divisible, verifiable, storable, fungible, salable, and globally recognized.

### The Network Effect Problem (and Why Bitcoin Solved It)

Anyone can copy Bitcoin's code and launch a new cryptocurrency. Thousands have. The question is whether they can acquire Bitcoin's:
- **Security** (hash rate)
- **Trust** (11+ years of unbroken protocol)
- **Adoption** (60%+ crypto market dominance)

Analogy: anyone can copy Wikipedia's text, but they can't copy the hundreds of millions of inbound links, top search rankings, or the community of editors.

### Bitcoin vs. Gold

Gold's demand is ~90% monetary/jewelry (network effect dependent), only ~10% industrial. Bitcoin is similar — its value derives from the same kind of network belief, but with superior portability and verifiability.

### Security Is Tied to Market Cap

Bitcoin's network security (hash rate) is tied to its market capitalization. More value → more mining incentive → more security → harder to attack. This creates a self-reinforcing moat that other cryptocurrencies cannot easily replicate.

Since 2017, Bitcoin's market share has stabilized above 60% despite thousands of competitors. The forks (Bitcoin Cash, BSV) failed to split the community meaningfully.

---

## Reason 2: The Halving Cycle

Bitcoin's protocol cuts the rate of new coin creation in half approximately every 4 years:

| Cycle | Halving Date | Reward Per Block |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| 1 (Launch) | Jan 2009 | 50 BTC |
| 2 | Nov 2012 | 25 BTC |
| 3 | Jul 2016 | 12.5 BTC |
| 4 | May 2020 | 6.25 BTC |
| 5 | Apr 2024 | 3.125 BTC |

After each halving: supply shock → price appreciation → speculative mania → correction → stabilization → next halving.

### Stock-to-Flow (S2F) Model

The **stock-to-flow ratio** compares stored supply to annual new supply:
- Gold: ~60x (60 years of production already above ground)
- Bitcoin post-2020 halving: ~56x → approaching gold parity
- Bitcoin post-2024 halving: >100x

The S2F model (by PlanB) back-tested well against historical cycles. Each cycle is less explosive than the prior one, but still directionally bullish.

**Important caveat:** The model captures supply mechanics well, but the demand side is unpredictable. My base case: bullish with new all-time highs within 2 years of halving, magnitude uncertain.

### Game Theory of Halving

With constant or growing demand flowing into a reduced supply of new coins, price pressure is structurally upward. Early buyers benefit from later buyers — but unlike a Ponzi, there is no promised return and no central organizer.

---

## Reason 3: Macro Context (Fiscal + Monetary Policy)

Written in July 2020, the macro backdrop included:

- **Federal Reserve** expanding balance sheet dramatically (QE)
- **US fiscal deficits** at multi-decade highs
- **Near-zero interest rates** globally, reducing the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets
- **Currency debasement** risk making "hard" assets more attractive

Bitcoin's fixed supply makes it a potential hedge against monetary debasement — a digital alternative to gold for storing value outside the traditional financial system.

Institutional adoption (MicroStrategy, Square, etc.) began in 2020, adding a new demand category that didn't exist in prior cycles.

---

## Key Risks to Monitor

1. **Demand-side miss:** If new adoption slows, the halving cycle plays out with less magnitude
2. **Regulatory crackdown:** Governments could restrict ownership or trading
3. **Quantum computing:** Eventually threatens current encryption (mitigatable with protocol updates)
4. **Market share loss:** Unlikely given current moat, but must be monitored
5. **Sentiment shift:** Gold survived because sentiment was sticky. Bitcoin needs the same durability.

---

## Summary

| Factor | Significance |
|--------|-------------|
| Hard 21M cap + network effect | Long-term store of value case |
| Halving cycle | ~4-year supply shock mechanism |
| Macro debasement environment | Short-to-medium term tailwind |
| Hash rate / security | Structural moat vs. competitors |

Bitcoin is best understood as a small position within a diversified portfolio, using capital you are willing to risk, held through high volatility without concern for periodic corrections.
